What is a polynomial?

What is a polynomial?

“Polynomial” literally means many terms.

Term

A term is a variable (like \(x\)) with a positive, whole number exponent and a number coefficient.

Ex: \(3x^2\)
Polynomial

A polynomial is an expression with multiple terms separated by \(+\) and \(-\).

Ex: \(3x^2-2x+1\)

Leading Term

The leading term of a polynomial contains the variable with the highest power.

It's good practice for this term to be first in the polynomial, and then order the rest of the terms in descending degree.

This can be called combining like terms.

Multiplying

Each term is multiplied by each term in the second term, using the Distributive Property repeatedly. Then combine like terms.

\( \begin{array}{l l}f(x) &= (3x^2 - 2x + 1) \cdot (2x + 3) \\ & = 3x^2 \cdot (2x + 3) - 2x \cdot (2x + 3) + 1 \cdot (2x + 3) \\ & = 6x^3 + 9x^2 - 4x^2 - 6x + 2x + 3 \\ & = 6x^3 + 5x^2 - 4x + 3 \end{array} \)

Perfect Squares: two identical binomials multiplied by each other

Difference of Squares: two similar binomials multiplied by each other with opposite signs
