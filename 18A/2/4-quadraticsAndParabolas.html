Completing the Square

  1. Identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), and then isolate \(c\)

  2. Calculate \(h=-b/2a\) \( = -2/(2\cdot 1) = \)\(-1\)

  3. Add \(h^2\) to BOTH sides

  4. Change the side with \(x\) into the squared form

  5. Move everything onto one side \(=0\)

You should get: \( a(x+h)^2 + c - h^2\)

\(0 = x^2 + 2x + 2\)

\(-2 = x^2 + 2x\)

Vertex Form
$$ a(x-h)^2+k $$
ABC Form
$$ ax^2+bx+c $$

Switching from ABC to Vertex

  1. Identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\)

  2. Calculate \(h=-b/2a\)

  3. Calculate \(k\) by plugging \(h\) into the ABC form

    The derivation for this is in the appendix.

  4. Plug \(a\), \(h\), and \(k\) into vertex form

Sketching Graphs

  1. Plot and label the vertex in the correct quadrant

    It does not need to be to scale! Do not draw a bunch of tick marks. Labeling the point with an ordered pair is enough.

  2. Plot and label the y-intercept

  3. Connect points with a parabola

    Check that the way it opens matches \(a\)!

Thinking Graphically

Roots actually show up in the parabola! There are 3 cases:

  • 2 real roots: hits \(x\)-axis twice

  • 1 double root: hits \(x\)-axis once

  • 2 imaginary roots: never hits \(x\)-axis

Thinking Graphically

This makes it easy to find the vertex!
If there’s one real root, that’s the vertex. But if there’s two...

  1. Find the distance between the two roots, and halve it.

  2. Find the \(x\) value of the midpoint.

  3. Plug in that \(x\) into the eq to find the \(y\). Done!
