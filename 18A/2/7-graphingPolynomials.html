Some Definitions

Polynomial (Review from 2.1)

A polynomial, meaning “multiple terms,” is a type of expression that only has terms with a variable (like \(x\)) with an exponent and a coefficient

Ex: \(7x^3+x+1\)
Degree (Review from 1.2)

the degree of a polynomial the highest exponent on the variable

Ex: \(3x^4 + 2x +7\) is degree 4
Leading Term (Review from 2.1)

the leading term is the term with the highest exponent

Ex: \(3x^4 + 2x +7\) has leading term \(3x^4\)
Leading Coefficient

the leading term’s coefficient is called the leading coefficient

Ex: \(3x^4 + 2x +7\) has leading coefficient \(3\)
Constant Term

a constant term is a term with no variable

Ex: \(3x^4 + 2x +7\) constant term is \(7\)
Descending Order

a polynomial is in descending order if the terms are arranged from highest to lowest degree

You can think of it as the exponents count down.
It’s ok if a number is skipped!
Ex: \(3x^4 + 2x +7\) is in descending order

Note

If we do “skip” an exponent, we can just say that term has coefficient \(0\).

$$ 3x^4 + {\color{#6fbaec} 0x^3 + 0x^2} + 2x +7 $$

End Behavior

the way \(y\) behaves as \(x\) goes to \(\pm \infty\) is called end behavior

  • “As \( x \rightarrow \infty\)” means we are looking at the Right Hand Side (RHS)

  • “As \( x \rightarrow -\infty\)” means we are looking at the Left Hand Side (LHS)

Zeroes of a Polynomial

What is the \(x\)-intercept?

There are multiple!

Real Zero

a real zero, or “root”, is an x-intercept of a polynomial (and there can be multiple!)

AKA, an \(x\) you plug in to make the polynomial \(=0\).

What is the real zero (x-intercept)?

\( y= x - 8\)        \( x=8\)
\( y= x + 2\)        \( x=-2\)
\( y= (x - 8)(x+2)\)        \( x=-2,8\)
Zero Product Property PAGE 37

The Zero Product Property (ZPP) states:

If \(A\cdot B=0\), then \(A=0\) and/or \(B=0\).

If we multiply several things and get zero,
at least one of them must be zero.
Ex: \((x-8)(x+2)=0\) means \(x-8=0\) and/or \(x+2=0\)

Working Backward

Finding Polynomials via Graphs

Given a graph, you can come with a reasonable equation. It might not be unique, but that's ok!

  1. Zeroes & Factors: every zero, or x-intercept, has a factor \( (x-\) zero\()\)

  2. Multiplicity: if it crosses like normal, no exponent on the factor

    • if it bounces, use an even degree (like \(2\))

    • if it squiggly crosses, use an odd exponent (like \(3,5,...\))

  3. End Behavior: if RHS goes down, put negative in front

Example 6
a

List the real zeroes.

\(x=-3, \frac{7}{4},9\)

b

Assuming all zeroes are real, what is a reasonable equation?

bounce at \(x=-3\), so \( (x+3)^2\)

normal at \(x=\frac{7}{4}\), so \( (x-\frac{7}{4})\)

bounce at \(x=9\), so \( (x-9)^2\)

RHS goes down, so put negative in front

\( -(x+3)^2(x-\frac{7}{4})(x-9)^2 \) \(=\) \(-(x+3)^2(4x-7)(x-9)^2\)
