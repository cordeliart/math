3.5 Algebra with Functions

Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying, & Dividing

We have shortahnds for operations with functions:

  • \( (f+g)(x) = f(x) + g(x) \)

  • \( (f-g)(x) = f(x) - g(x) \)

  • \( (fg) (x) = f(x) \cdot g(x) \)

  • \( \left(\frac{f}{g}\right)(x) = f(x) / g(x)\) where \(g(x)\neq 0\)

Example 1

Let \(f(x) = x + 3\) and let \( g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x + 3} \).

a

\( (f+g)(x) \)

\( = x + 3 + \frac{1}{x+3}\)

b

\( (f-g)(x) \)

\( = x + 3 - \frac{1}{x+3}\)

c

\( (fg)(x) \)

\( = (x + 3) \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x+3} \right) = 1\) with \(x\neq -3\)

d

\( \left(\frac{f}{g}\right)(x) \)

\( = (x + 3)\div \left( \frac{1}{x+3} \right) = (x + 3)\cdot \left( \frac{x+3}{1} \right) = x^2 + 6x + 9\)

Domain Danger Zones

Domain needs to determined before simplifying. When we cancel out a denominator, we can lose track of our danger zones.

So, anything left out of the domain for \(f(x)\) or \(g(x)\) must also be left out for the domain of \((fg)(x)\).

Domain Danger Zones

Example 2, we had:

\( f(x) = x+3\)

\( g(x) = \frac{1}{x+3} \)

  ->     \( (fg)(x)=1 \)

If we just look at \((fg)(x)\), we can’t see the danger zone in \(g\). This is why we need to find domain first.

When to Plug In

You might be asked to plug values in before combining functions.

In that case, plug in your inputs, and then combine the outputs. You cannot combine inputs first!!

$$ f(a) + f(b) \neq f(a+b) $$

You can consider this an order of operations thing. Inputs become outputs first, and then PEMDAS.

Composition

Function composition is applying functions in sequential order, working from inside out. In other words, a function is an input for another function. Order matters!

  • \( (f\circ g)(x) = f \big( g(x) \big) \), which is read "\(f\) of \(g\) of \(x\)"

  • \( (g\circ f)(x) = g \big( f(x) \big) \), which is read "\(g\) of \(f\) of \(x\)"

$$ (f\circ {\color{#386ef6}g})(x) = f\big( {\color{#386ef6}g(x)} \big)$$

We call the leftmost function the outer function, and the rightmost one the inner function.

The inner function is ALWAYS applied first.
