4.3 Properties of Log

Starting onPg. 135

Recall

Inverses cancel when composed:

\( (f\circ f^{-1}) (x) = x\)

\( (f^{-1} \circ f)(x) = x\)

Logs are defined as the inverses of exponentials:

\( f(x) = b^x \) gives us \( f^{-1}(x) = \log_b(x) \)

-> so they cancel each other out!

\( (f\circ f^{-1}) (x) = b^{\log_b(x)} = x \)

\( (f^{-1}\circ f) (x) = \log_b(b^x) = x \)

Exponentiate

exponentiating is when both sides of the equation become the exponents on the same base \(b\)

Useful for cancelling logarithms!!

\(x=y \phantom{"} \longleftrightarrow \phantom{"} b^x = b^y \)

Solving for \(x\) Trapped in a Logarithm

  1. Condense into single log, if needed.

  2. Isolate single on one side if, needed.

  3. Exponentiate both sides (using the SAME BASE as the logarithm) to cancel.

  4. Solve for \(x\) as usual.

  5. Check your answer in the original. (Extraneous solutions!!)
