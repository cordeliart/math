4.4 Solving Log & Exponential Equations

Starting onPg. 135

Recall

Inverses cancel each other out.

\( {\color{#d10808}(f\circ f^{-1}) ({\color{#111111}x})} = x \)

\( {\color{#d10808}(f^{-1}\circ f) ({\color{#111111}x})} = x \)

Recall

Exponentials and logs are inverses (by definition), so they cancel each other out.

\( {\color{#d10808}b^{\log_b({\color{#111111}x})} } = x \)

\( {\color{#d10808}\log_b(b^{\color{#111111}x}) } = x \)

When \(x\) is in a Logarithm

Exponentiate

exponentiating is when both sides of the equation become the exponents on the same base \(b\)

Useful for cancelling logarithms!!

\(x=y\)   ->   \( b^x = b^y \)

Solving for \(x\) Trapped in a Logarithm

  1. Condense into single log, if needed.

  2. Isolate single log on one side if, needed.

  3. Exponentiate both sides (using the SAME BASE as the logarithm) to cancel.

  4. Solve for \(x\) as usual.

  5. Check your answer in the original. (Extraneous solutions!!)

When \(x\) is in an Exponents

Rules for Logarithms (Review from 4.3)
Power Rule    power -> multiple

\( \log_b(A^B) = B\log_b(A) \)

Solving for \(x\) Trapped in an Exponent

  1. Condense into single exponential, if needed.

  2. Isolate single exponential on one side if, needed.

  3. Take natural log of both sides. (Natural log is easier to put into calculators!) *

  4. Apply the power rule to take out the exponent with \(x\). Use parentheses to be safe!!

  5. Finish solving for \(x\) as usual.

Solving for \(x\) Trapped in an Exponent on Base \(e\)

Natural log and natural base just cancel!

$$ e^{(...)} = \#$$
$$ ln \left( {\color{#111111}e^{(...)}} \right) = ln({\color{#111111}\#})$$
$$(...) = ln(\#)$$

Remember, \( ln(\#) \) is just a number! Plug it into your calculator to get your final answer.

Solving for \(x\) Trapped in an Exponent on Base \(10\)

Common log and base 10 just cancel!

$$ 10^{(...)} = \#$$
$$ \log \left( {\color{#111111}10^{(...)}} \right) = \log({\color{#111111}\#})$$
$$(...) = \log(\#)$$

Remember, \( \log(\#) \) is just a number! Plug it into your calculator to get your final answer.

Example 4

Solve \( 5^{x-7}=25 \) for \(x\).

Take natural log

\( ln(5^{x-7}) = ln(25)\)

Power rule

\( (x-7) \cdot ln(5) = ln(25)\)

Solve

\( (x-7) = \dfrac{ln(25)}{ln(5)}\)

\( x = \dfrac{ln(25)}{ln(5)} + 7 \)

\( x = 9 \)

Example 5

Solve \( 7^{x}=31 \) for \(x\), rounded to 4 decimal places.

Take natural log

\( ln(7^{x}) = ln(31)\)

Power rule

\( x \cdot ln(7) = ln(31)\)

Solve

\( x = \dfrac{ln(31)}{ln(7)}\)

\( x \approx 1.7647 \)
